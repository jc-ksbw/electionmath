Monterey County

What’s left to count?

Local single-seat contests and ballot measures in Monterey County — county, city, school and special-district. For every race still undecided, see how many of the outstanding ballots the trailing candidate would need.

Which contests are here. Every local contest that elects one person, plus local ballot measures. Contests electing two or more — city councils electing two, school boards electing three or five — are left out. In those a voter casts several votes, so one ballot can back both a trailing candidate and the one they would have to pass; a “share of the remaining ballots” figure assumes a gain for one is a loss for the other, and that is not true there. Full results for those contests are on the county’s own page, linked above.

How the estimate works. The countywide total of ballots still to be counted is the figure reported by the Registrar. Each contest reports its own registration and turnout, so ballots already counted in that jurisdiction = registration × reported turnout, and the outstanding ballots are spread across jurisdictions in proportion to those counts. The countywide number is known; the split between jurisdictions is an estimate. For the top candidate not currently winning a seat, we show the votes needed to pass the last candidate in, and what share of that jurisdiction’s outstanding ballots it would take — assuming those ballots split only between those two candidates. It is a scenario, not a forecast of how anyone will vote.

Caveats. Results are unofficial until certified by the Monterey County Registrar of Voters. Outstanding ballots are not evenly spread, and not every ballot carries a vote in every contest. Some measures need a 55% or two-thirds majority to pass — this page shows which side leads, not whether a measure clears its threshold.
